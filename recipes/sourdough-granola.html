Sourdough Granola

Recipe by Molly Marzalek-Kelly · King Arthur Baking · sent by Melissa, August 2026

Melissa forwarded this one asking, "Should we try making our own granola?" Ripe or discard sourdough starter stands in for the usual butter-and-egg binder, acting as glue for oats, puffed rice, nuts, and seeds while cutting down on added sweetener. Bake it into clusters, then stir in dried fruit once it's cooled — it keeps for a month in an airtight container.

~40 mintotal time
15 minprep
Vegandiet
4½ cupsyield

Ingredients

  • ⅓ cup (76g) sourdough starter, ripe or discard
  • 4–6 tbsp (78–117g) maple syrup
  • 1 tbsp (13g) vegetable oil
  • ¼ tsp table salt
  • 1 tsp ground cinnamon
  • 1 cup (113g) rolled oats
  • ¾ cup (112g) puffed rice
  • ½ cup (57g) pecan halves, coarsely chopped
  • ¼ cup (35g) sunflower seeds
  • ¼ cup (41g) pumpkin seeds
  • 1 tbsp flax seeds (optional)
  • 1 tbsp chia seeds (optional)
  • ⅓ cup (28g) unsweetened shredded coconut
  • ½ cup (70–80g) dried fruit — cherries, raisins, currants, blueberries, or dates

Method

  1. Preheat. Set oven to 325°F. Lightly grease or line a large rimmed baking sheet with parchment.
  2. Mix the wet ingredients. Combine sourdough starter, maple syrup, oil, salt, and cinnamon in a small bowl until smooth.
  3. Mix the dry ingredients. In a large bowl, stir together oats, puffed rice, pecans, sunflower and pumpkin seeds, flax and chia seeds, and coconut.
  4. Combine. Pour the wet mixture into the dry ingredients and stir until everything is evenly moistened.
  5. Bake. Spread onto the prepared baking sheet, clustering the mixture as desired. Bake 25–30 minutes, until golden brown and fragrant.
  6. Finish. Cool completely, then stir in the dried fruit. Store airtight for a month or more.
