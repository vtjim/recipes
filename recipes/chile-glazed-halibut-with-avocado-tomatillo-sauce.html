Chile-Glazed Halibut
with Avocado-Tomatillo Sauce

Recipe by Barbara Pool Fenzl, Bon Appétit, July 2006 · Epicurious · sent by Melissa, April 2012

Melissa passed this one along from Epicurious's "send to a friend" feature. A sweet-smoky chipotle-honey glaze brushed into slits cut across halibut fillets, grilled with orange slices tucked in, and served over a cool, creamy avocado-tomatillo sauce.

6servings
Grilledmethod
~30 minactive time

Ingredients

  • Glaze
  • 6 tbsp fresh orange juice
  • 6 tbsp honey
  • 1½ tsp minced canned chipotle chiles
  • 1 garlic clove, coarsely chopped
  • ¼ tsp ground cinnamon
  • Sauce
  • 1 large avocado, halved, pitted, peeled
  • 2 medium tomatillos (about 4 oz), husked, rinsed, coarsely chopped
  • ¼ cup fresh orange juice
  • ¼ tsp (or more) hot pepper sauce
  • Fish
  • 6 (5 oz) halibut fillets, each about 1 inch thick
  • 1 orange, skin on, cut lengthwise in half, thinly sliced crosswise
  • Ground cumin, to taste
  • Salt and pepper, to taste

Method

  1. Make the glaze. Blend all glaze ingredients until smooth. Season with salt and pepper. Can be made 1 day ahead; cover and chill, stir before using.
  2. Make the sauce. Combine all sauce ingredients in a blender and blend until smooth. Can be made 2 hours ahead; cover and let stand at room temperature.
  3. Prep the fish. Prepare a barbecue for medium heat. Make crosswise slits in each fillet, cutting to within ¼ inch of the bottom, spacing slits about ¾ inch apart. Brush glaze over the top and into the slits. Tuck orange slices into the slits. Sprinkle with cumin, salt, and pepper.
  4. Grill. Place fish orange-side up on the grill, cover, and cook until opaque, about 8 minutes. Carefully transfer to a platter with a wide spatula and let rest a few minutes.
  5. Plate. Spoon the avocado sauce onto plates, spreading slightly. Arrange one fillet over the sauce on each plate.

Chipotle chiles are dried, smoked jalapeños canned in adobo sauce, found at supermarkets, specialty stores, or Latin markets.

Tomatillos are green, tomato-like fruits with a papery husk, sold in the produce section or at Latin markets.
